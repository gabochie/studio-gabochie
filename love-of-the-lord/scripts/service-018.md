## word
Full sermon today: Citizens, Not Consumers. Open your Bible to Philippians 3, verse 20. For our citizenship is in heaven, from which we also eagerly wait for the Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ. You carry two addresses: a house in Ghana and a homeland in heaven. Today we learn to live like it.

Paul wrote "citizenship" — politeuma — to people who understood Rome. Roman citizens had rights, protection, and allegiance no matter where they lived. Philippi itself was a Roman colony: Roman law, Roman customs, Roman pride, hundreds of miles from Rome. That is exactly what the church is: a colony of heaven. We live HERE but we belong THERE — and our colony should look, sound, and love like home.

So stop living as a CONSUMER. Consumers ask: what do I get? Citizens ask: what do I build? Consumers church-hop for better music; citizens plant and water. Consumers dodge tax, cut queues, and bribe; citizens model integrity that shames corruption. Consumers use people; citizens serve them. Look at your week: did you mostly take or mostly give? Consumers drain colonies; citizens develop them.

Because citizens are AMBASSADORS. An ambassador represents his king's culture in a foreign land — speech, dress, dealing, dignity. When you enter a room, heaven should enter with you: truth on your lips, peace in your chest, love in your hands. People should taste the Kingdom in how you treat waiters, juniors, rivals, and the poor. You are heaven's embassy in your street. Keep it open daily.

And note the contrast Paul draws just before: enemies of the cross, whose god is their belly, who mind earthly things. Belly-god religion — appetite as lord, cravings as compass. Citizens mind HEAVENLY things while handling earthly ones well. Same markets, different masters. Same Ghana, different governor.

Your step this month: pick one citizen-act weekly — serve somewhere unnoticed, refuse one corrupt shortcut, give to one needy cause, invite one person to church. And declare your allegiance afresh today: I belong to heaven first, Ghana second, self last.

Father, my citizenship is in heaven. Make me a builder, not a consumer; an ambassador, not a tourist. Let my street taste Your Kingdom through my life. In Jesus' name. Amen.
