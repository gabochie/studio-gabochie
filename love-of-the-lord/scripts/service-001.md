## word
Full sermon today: Grace for New Beginnings. Open your Bible to Lamentations chapter three, verses twenty-two to twenty-three. The steadfast love of the Lord never ceases. His mercies never come to an end. They are new every morning. Great is His faithfulness.

Stop and feel the weight of where these words were written. Lamentations is a funeral song. Jerusalem has fallen. The walls are rubble, the temple is ash, the people are in chains. The poet is sitting in the ruins of everything he loved. And in the middle of chapter three — the middle of the ashes — he stops and says: yet this I call to mind, and therefore I have hope. Because of the Lord's great love we are not consumed.

Point one: new beginnings are born in ruins, not in comfort. We keep waiting for life to be tidy before we start again — the debt cleared, the apology accepted, the fear gone. But God does not wait for tidy. He met Israel in rubble. He met Peter after the denial, on a beach, cooking breakfast — not with a lecture, but with a question: do you love Me? Your ruin is not disqualification. It is the address where mercy delivers. Whatever ended last season — a job, a relationship, your confidence — that ending is the doorway, not the grave.

Point two: mercy is daily bread, not stored grain. Notice the rhythm: new every morning. Not new every year. Not new when you finally get it right. Every single morning. The Israelites could not hoard manna; it rotted if they tried. Mercy works the same — you cannot live today on last month's mercy, and you do not need to. There is fresh supply this morning with your name on it. So stop dragging yesterday's failure into today's account. Yesterday's mercy covered yesterday. Today's mercy is already issued. Receive it like breakfast: daily, gratefully, without earning it.

Point three: faithfulness is God's character, not your reward. Great is Thy faithfulness — not great is my performance. The anchor of a new beginning is not your resolve; it is His nature. You will wobble this week. You will forget by Wednesday. His faithfulness does not fluctuate with your feelings. Build on rock, not on mood.

Here is your step for the week — one thing. Write down one honest new beginning: one apology to make, one habit to start, one step of obedience you have been postponing. Write it, tell one person, and do it before Friday. Beginnings die in secrecy and live in the light.

Father, we receive fresh mercy this morning. Wash yesterday away. Give every listener one clear beginning and the courage to take it. Plant this word deep and make it fruit this week. In Jesus' name. Amen.
