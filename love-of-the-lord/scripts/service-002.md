## word
Refresh today: Mercy That Never Runs Out. Open your Bible to Psalm 103, verses 8 to 12. Last week we began again. This week we learn why beginning again will never run out.

David writes: The Lord is compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, abounding in love. He will not always accuse, nor will He harbor His anger forever. He does not treat us as our sins deserve or repay us according to our iniquities. For as high as the heavens are above the earth, so great is His love for those who fear Him. As far as the east is from the west, so far has He removed our transgressions from us.

Point one: mercy is His NATURE, not His mood. Compassionate and gracious — that is the name God proclaimed to Moses on the mountain. You do not catch God on a good day. Mercy is who He is every day, including the Monday after you failed on Sunday. Stop reading His face through your feelings. Read His name instead.

Point two: mercy REMOVES, it does not just cover. East from west — a distance you can never cross twice. God does not keep your sin in a drawer to bring out in an argument. Confessed sin is removed sin. If you are still serving a sentence for what you already confessed, you are sitting in a prison God did not build. Walk out of it today.

Point three: mercy PITIES your dust. As a father has compassion on his children — and He remembers that we are dust. Dust! Your weakness does not disgust God; it moves Him the way a father is moved by a stumbling child. Bring Him the weakness, not the performance.

Here is your step for the week. Write down the one failure you keep replaying. Pray over it once, out loud: Father, I receive mercy for this. Then tear the paper or delete the note — east from west. And extend the same mercy to one person this week, because mercy received but never given goes stale.

Father, thank You for mercy that outlasts my mistakes. I receive it without suspicion, and I will give it without resentment. Plant this word deep and make it fruit. In Jesus' name. Amen.
