CV & Cover Letter Guide

Your CV is often the first impression you make on an employer. In Ghana's competitive job market, a well-crafted CV can be the difference between getting an interview and being overlooked. This guide walks you through building a CV that gets results.

1. Choose the Right Format

For most professionals in Ghana, a reverse-chronological CV works best. List your most recent experience first and work backward. Use a functional CV only if you're changing industries or have significant gaps in employment.

2. Essential Sections

  • Contact Information — Full name, phone number (include WhatsApp), professional email, LinkedIn profile, and location (city/region).
  • Professional Summary — A 2-3 sentence overview of who you are, your key skills, and what you're looking for. Tailor this to each role.
  • Work Experience — For each role, include job title, company/organization, dates (month/year), and 3-5 bullet points of achievements. Use numbers where possible: "Increased social media engagement by 40% in 3 months."
  • Education — List your highest qualification first. Include institution name, degree/certificate, and year of completion.
  • Skills — Both hard skills (software, languages, technical abilities) and soft skills (communication, leadership, problem-solving).
  • References — "Available on request" is acceptable. Have at least 2 professional referees ready.

3. Ghana-Specific Tips

  • Include your WhatsApp number — Many employers in Ghana prefer WhatsApp for initial contact.
  • One page is ideal for graduates with less than 5 years experience. Two pages max for senior roles.
  • Save as PDF — Word documents can lose formatting across devices. Always submit a PDF unless asked otherwise.
  • Use a professional email — firstname.lastname@gmail.com, not partyboy96@yahoo.com.
  • National Service — Include your NSS placement if applicable. It counts as work experience.
Template Tip: Use the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for your bullet points. Instead of "Responsible for social media," write "Developed a 30-day content strategy that grew TikTok followers from 500 to 2,000."

4. Cover Letter Essentials

A cover letter is your chance to tell a story. Keep it to one page and follow this structure:

  • Opening — State the role you're applying for and where you saw the listing.
  • Body paragraph 1 — Why you're a great fit. Connect your skills to the job requirements.
  • Body paragraph 2 — Why you want to work for this organization. Show you've done your research.
  • Closing — Thank them and express enthusiasm for an interview.

5. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Typos and grammatical errors — proofread, then proofread again. Use Grammarly if needed.
  • Using the same CV for every application — customize for each role.
  • Including irrelevant information — your primary school details don't belong on a professional CV.
  • Using unprofessional fonts or colors — stick to clean, simple designs.

Interview Preparation

Interviews can be nerve-wracking, but preparation is the antidote to anxiety. Whether it's your first job interview or your tenth, this guide will help you show up confident and prepared.

1. Before the Interview

  • Research the company — Understand their mission, products/services, recent news, and company culture. Look at their website, social media, and any news articles.
  • Review the job description — Identify the top 5 requirements and prepare specific examples that demonstrate each one.
  • Prepare your answers — Practice common questions (see below) out loud. Record yourself and listen back.
  • Prepare questions to ask — Smart questions show you're genuinely interested. Ask about team culture, growth opportunities, or current projects.
  • Logistics — Confirm the time, location (or video link), and format. Plan to arrive 15 minutes early. For video interviews, test your camera, microphone, and internet connection.

2. Common Questions & How to Answer

"Tell me about yourself." — Give a 60-second summary: where you are now, relevant experience, and why you're excited about this role. Don't recite your entire life story.

"What are your strengths and weaknesses?" — Pick strengths relevant to the role. For weaknesses, choose something you're actively working to improve and explain how.

"Why do you want to work here?" — Connect the company's mission to your personal values. Show you've done your research.

"Where do you see yourself in 5 years?" — Show ambition but stay realistic. "I want to grow into a senior role where I can mentor others" is a solid answer.

"Tell me about a challenge you overcame." — Use the STAR method: Situation, Task, Action, Result.

3. Dress Code in Ghana

For corporate roles (banking, finance, law, insurance): wear a suit or formal business attire. For creative roles (tech, media, marketing): smart casual is often acceptable. When in doubt, overdress. It's better to be the best-dressed person in the room than the worst.

4. During the Interview

  • Body language — Sit up straight, maintain eye contact, smile, and nod to show you're listening.
  • Speak clearly — Don't rush. Pause to collect your thoughts before answering difficult questions.
  • Be honest — If you don't know something, say so, but show willingness to learn.
  • Take notes — Bring a small notebook and pen. It shows preparedness.
Pro Tip: Always send a thank-you email within 24 hours of the interview. Keep it brief: thank them for their time, reiterate your interest, and mention one specific point from the conversation that excited you.

5. Following Up

If you haven't heard back within the timeline they mentioned, send a polite follow-up email. Don't call repeatedly or show up at the office. Patience and professionalism leave a lasting positive impression.

Freelance & Gig Economy

Ghana's digital economy is growing rapidly, and freelancing offers an accessible path to earning a sustainable income. Whether you're a student, a recent graduate, or a professional looking for side income, this guide will help you get started.

1. In-Demand Skills

  • Writing & Content — Copywriting, content writing, ghostwriting, editing, proofreading. Platforms like Upwork and Fiverr have high demand for English writers.
  • Design — Graphic design (Canva, Adobe Suite), UI/UX design (Figma), video editing. Ghanaian businesses need branding materials.
  • Development — Web development (HTML/CSS/JavaScript), mobile apps (Flutter), WordPress, Shopify. Tech skills command premium rates.
  • Digital Marketing — Social media management, SEO, Google Ads, email marketing. Many SMEs lack digital expertise.
  • Virtual Assistance — Calendar management, email handling, data entry, customer support. Remote companies hire VAs from Ghana.
  • Transcription & Translation — English/Twi, English/Hausa, or other Ghanaian language pairs are valuable.

2. Best Platforms for Ghanaian Freelancers

  • Upwork — The largest freelance marketplace. Create a strong profile and start with smaller jobs to build reviews.
  • Fiverr — Good for offering specific services (gigs) at set prices. Great for design, writing, and video work.
  • PeoplePerHour — Popular with UK and European clients. Good for creative and technical services.
  • Toptal — For experienced developers and designers. Higher rates but tougher to get accepted.
  • Local Platforms — Check LinkedIn, Jobberman Ghana, and even Facebook groups for local freelance opportunities.

3. Setting Your Rates

Research what others charge for similar services. As a beginner, you may need to start lower to build a portfolio, but don't undervalue yourself. In 2026, a good starting range for Ghanaian freelancers is:

  • Writing: GHS 50-200 per article (or $10-40 on international platforms)
  • Design: GHS 150-500 per project
  • Development: GHS 300-1500+ per project
  • Virtual Assistance: $3-8 per hour on international platforms

4. Getting Your First Clients

  • Portfolio is everything — Before clients trust you, they need to see your work. Create sample projects if you don't have real ones yet.
  • Start with smaller jobs — Take lower-paying work at first to build reviews and social proof.
  • Network actively — Join Ghanaian freelancer communities on WhatsApp, Telegram, and LinkedIn.
  • Ask for referrals — Satisfied clients are your best marketing. Don't be shy to ask for recommendations.
Getting Paid: For international clients, use PayPal (withdraw to your Ghanaian bank via GTBank/Ecobank), Payoneer, or Wise. For local clients, Mobile Money (MoMo) is the most convenient option. Always agree on payment terms upfront — 50% deposit is standard for larger projects.

5. Managing Your Freelance Business

  • Track your income and expenses — Use a simple spreadsheet or an app like Wave. Set aside money for taxes.
  • Create contracts — Even a simple email agreement outlining scope, timeline, and payment terms protects both parties.
  • Deliver on time — Reliability is your biggest competitive advantage. Meet every deadline.
  • Keep learning — The market evolves. Invest in courses to upgrade your skills regularly.

Networking Guide

In Ghana and across Africa, relationships open doors. A strong professional network can lead to job opportunities, mentorship, partnerships, and career growth. Here's how to build yours strategically.

1. Start with LinkedIn

LinkedIn is the most powerful professional networking tool available. Optimize your profile:

  • Professional photo — A clear, well-lit headshot. You don't need a studio; a smartphone photo against a plain wall works.
  • Strong headline — Don't just put "Student" or "Job Seeker." Write what you do and what you're passionate about: "Aspiring Software Developer | Building solutions for Ghana's digital future."
  • About section — Tell your story in 3-4 paragraphs. Include your skills, what you're learning, and what kind of opportunities you're seeking.
  • Connect intentionally — Don't spam connection requests. Personalize each one: "Hi [Name], I admire your work in [field] and would love to connect and learn from your journey."

2. Attend Events

Ghana has a vibrant professional events scene:

  • Tech events — Ghana Tech Summit, DevCongress, Mobile Web Ghana meetups, Accra Tech Fest.
  • Creative events — Chale Wote Street Art Festival, Accra Design Week, poetry slams and open mics.
  • Business events — Ghana CEO Summit, AGI events, chamber of commerce networking sessions.
  • Online webinars — Many international organizations host free webinars. Attend, participate, and follow up with speakers.

When attending events, set a goal: "I'll have 3 meaningful conversations and collect 5 business cards." Quality over quantity.

3. Find a Mentor

A mentor can accelerate your growth by sharing experience, providing feedback, and opening doors. Here's how to find one:

  • Identify people you admire — Look at professionals 5-10 years ahead of you in your field.
  • Engage with their content — Comment thoughtfully on their LinkedIn posts. Share their articles.
  • Make a specific ask — "I'd love 15 minutes of your time to ask about your journey in [field]. Would you be open to a quick chat?"
  • Come prepared — Have specific questions. Respect their time. Always send a thank-you note.
  • Pay it forward — When you're established, mentor someone else. That's how communities grow.

4. Join Professional Communities

  • WhatsApp & Telegram Groups — Join groups related to your field. Be active, share value, and avoid spamming.
  • Professional Associations — Institute of ICT Professionals (IIPGH), Ghana Institute of Engineers, Ghana Bar Association.
  • Alumni Networks — Your university's alumni association is an instant network. Join and participate.
The 80/20 Rule of Networking: Give 80% of the value, ask for 20%. Share resources, introduce people, celebrate others' wins. When you give first, people naturally want to help you in return.

5. Networking Etiquette in Ghana

  • Always greet with respect — "Good morning/sir/madam" goes a long way.
  • Exchange contact cards — Still common and appreciated in formal settings.
  • Follow up within 48 hours — "It was great meeting you at [event]. I'd love to stay connected."
  • Don't ask for a job immediately — Build the relationship first. Opportunities will come naturally.
  • Be reliable — If you promise to send something, send it. Your reputation is your currency.

Remote Work Guide

Remote work is transforming the global job market, and Ghanaian professionals are increasingly competing for and landing international roles. This guide shows you how to position yourself for remote opportunities.

1. Why Remote Work?

Remote jobs offer several advantages for Ghanaian professionals: competitive USD salaries, flexibility, exposure to global standards, and the ability to work from anywhere with a good internet connection. Many remote roles pay 3-10x what local companies offer for similar work.

2. Top Platforms for Remote Jobs

  • Remote OK — Curated remote jobs across all industries. Filter by timezone and skill.
  • We Work Remotely — One of the largest remote job boards with categories for development, design, sales, and more.
  • FlexJobs — Hand-screened remote and flexible jobs. Requires a subscription but has fewer scams.
  • LinkedIn — Use the "Remote" filter when searching jobs. Many companies now list remote-first positions.
  • AngelList / Wellfound — Startup jobs, many of which are remote-first. Great for tech roles.
  • Indeed — Filter by "Remote" under location. Widely used by companies of all sizes.

3. Essential Tools

  • Reliable Internet — Invest in a good MiFi or fiber connection. Have a backup (e.g., mobile data).
  • Communication — Slack, Microsoft Teams, Zoom, Google Meet. Master professional communication on these platforms.
  • Project Management — Trello, Asana, Notion, Jira. Familiarize yourself with at least one.
  • Time Management — Toggl, RescueTime, or a simple Pomodoro timer. Remote work requires self-discipline.
  • Payment — PayPal, Payoneer, Wise, or direct bank transfer. Have at least two options set up.

4. Standing Out as a Ghanaian Candidate

  • Excellent English — Many remote roles require strong written communication. Proofread everything.
  • Reliable schedule — International teams value dependability. Show up on time for every meeting.
  • Over-communicate — In remote work, silence is interpreted as lack of progress. Send regular updates.
  • Professional workspace — Invest in a decent microphone and webcam. A quiet, professional background matters.
  • Highlight timezone advantages — "I'm in UTC+0, which overlaps with both US and European business hours" is a selling point.

5. Common Challenges & Solutions

  • Internet outages — Have a backup plan. A smartphone hotspot can save you in a pinch.
  • Power cuts — Invest in a UPS (uninterruptible power supply) for your router and laptop.
  • Payment delays — Build a 3-month emergency fund so one late payment doesn't derail you.
  • Isolation — Join co-working spaces in Accra, Kumasi, or Takoradi. The Gravity Co-work and Impact Hub Accra are good options.
  • Currency fluctuation — Consider saving a portion of your income in USD or stablecoins to protect against cedi depreciation.
Getting Started: If you're new to remote work, start with a part-time or contract role to build experience. Join communities like "African Remote Workers" on Facebook and "Remote Africa" on WhatsApp to learn from others' experiences.
